# The patterns

Six chapters on what the course website was doing during class, and how to run
each of those things without it. Five are classroom procedures. The sixth is a
decision about third-party tools.

## What these are, and what they are not

**None of these procedures has been run with students.**

The course ran on the software for a full semester, so every one of these
chapters describes a translation rather than a practice. The software versions
are what has classroom evidence behind them: the AI sorted the submissions, the
AI facilitated the group discussions, the AI checked the reading. What you have
here is the same pedagogical job, worked out for cards and a whiteboard, by
someone who watched the software do it twenty-six times and has strong opinions
about what it was actually for.

That distinction matters for how you read the numbers. When a chapter says four
seconds a ticket, ninety seconds a runner, or twelve to fifteen minutes for a
gallery walk, those are estimates from someone who knows the room, not
measurements. The pacing chapter holds the timing data to a standard that
discards 24 of 26 recorded stages; held to that same standard, the numbers in
these chapters would all be discarded, because there was never an instrument
pointed at them.

What is evidenced, and worth the confidence it is written with:

- What each software component was doing, and why. That came from building it
  and teaching with it.
- What was lost when it did that job. Every chapter has a "what you lose"
  section, and those are the most reliable paragraphs here.
- The design reasoning connecting the two.

If you run one of these and it goes differently, that is the expected outcome
and the author would rather hear it than not.
