# AI and Work

**Instructor Guide** · 2026-04-02

Pair discussion of how AI is changing work, who benefits, and what should be done

## Facilitation overview

## Facilitation Notes

**Philosophy:** Four rounds of paired discussion exploring AI's impact on work from personal experience to policy. The arc moves from concrete (what did you experience?) to analytical (who survives?) to values-based (who decides?). Tuesday's data analysis lab gives students direct experience to draw on. Share-outs are substantial (10 min each) to surface disagreements and connect themes.

**Logistics:**
- Four discussion rounds with new partners each time (4 GroupActivity blocks with the same `group` key)
- Each round has a 10-minute discussion followed by a 10-minute share-out
- No roles. One partner should capture key points in the TextSubmission each round
- Rounds 2 and 4 include agent-guided interaction
- The share-out stages are instructor-led: ask 2-3 pairs to share, then connect to the next round's theme

**Pacing:**
| Stage | Duration | Cumulative |
|-------|----------|------------|
| Round 1: Your Own Experience | 10m | 10m |
| Round 1: Share Out | 10m | 20m |
| Round 2: What's Actually Happening | 10m | 30m |
| Round 2: Share Out | 10m | 40m |
| Round 3: Horse or Coal? | 10m | 50m |
| Round 3: Share Out | 10m | 60m |
| Round 4: Who Decides? | 10m | 70m |
| Round 4: Share Out | 10m | 80m |

**Tips:**
- Round 1 is the warmup. Students should be drawing on Tuesday's lab experience. If they haven't used AI in other contexts, push them to reflect on the data analysis lab specifically.
- Round 2 is where the Thompson piece comes alive. The key tension: individual happiness vs systemic displacement. Don't let pairs settle on one side.
- Round 3 gets personal. Students applying the horse-vs-coal test to their own careers can feel vulnerable. Create space for honest uncertainty.
- Round 4 often reveals that students haven't thought about governance at all. That's fine. The question is the point.
- During share-outs, look for pairs who disagreed with each other.

## Activity

### Stage 1: Round 1: Your Own Experience

**Timing:** planned 10m

_Partners discuss their experience using AI as a tool_

> **Facilitation notes**
>
> This round grounds the discussion in direct experience. Every student just did the data analysis lab on Tuesday. Push them to be specific: what did AI do well? Where did it struggle? Did they feel like they were learning or just prompting? If students have used AI in jobs or internships, draw that out.

**Grouping:** 2 per group

#### Your Own Experience

On Tuesday you used AI to analyze data, create visualizations, and present findings. Think about that experience, and any other times you've used AI for work or school.

**Discuss with your partner:** How did using AI for data analysis feel? Did it make you more capable, or did you feel like you were outsourcing your thinking? Have you used AI for work in other classes or jobs? When AI does something well, does that feel empowering or threatening?

**Capture your key points:**

_Expected response: What did you and your partner discuss? Where did you agree or disagree?_

### Stage 2: Round 1: Share Out

**Timing:** planned 10m

_Class reconvenes to share observations_

> **Facilitation notes**
>
> Ask 2-3 pairs to share. Listen for the tension between empowerment and displacement. Bridge to Round 2: "You've experienced AI as a tool for one afternoon. The coders in Thompson's piece live this every day. Let's look at what happens when AI becomes your entire work life."

#### Share Out

Your instructor will ask a few pairs to share what they discussed. Listen for ideas that challenge or extend your own thinking.

### Stage 3: Round 2: What's Actually Happening

**Timing:** planned 10m

_New partners discuss how AI is changing work right now_

> **Facilitation notes**
>
> This round centers on Thompson's reporting. The key contradiction: individual coders are happier and more productive, but Block fired 4,000 people. Both things are true. Push pairs to engage with Lowrey's framework: are Thompson's happy coders "coal" (temporarily safe, even thriving) or are they next in line to be "horses" (the AI agents they manage will eventually manage themselves)?

**Grouping:** 2 per group

#### What's Actually Happening to Work

Thompson's coders say they're happier and more productive with AI. They spend less time on tedious boilerplate and more on interesting problems. But Jack Dorsey fired half of Block's employees because AI tools paired with smaller teams enabled "a new way of working."

Lowrey offers a framework: are you a horse (permanently displaced by a better technology) or coal (temporarily disrupted but ultimately in greater demand because efficiency drives new uses)?

**Discuss with your partner:** Can both things be true: workers happier AND expendable? Who in Thompson's piece is a "horse" and who is "coal"? What determines which?

### Stage 5: Round 3: Horse or Coal?

**Timing:** planned 10m

_New partners apply the framework to their own futures_

> **Facilitation notes**
>
> This round gets personal. Students applying the horse-vs-coal test to their own career plans can feel vulnerable. Encourage honesty. Key insight from Lowrey: it's not just about skill. Radiologists survived partly because of FDA regulation. Factors beyond individual capability matter: regulation, professional licensing, unions, consumer preferences, cultural values. If a pair focuses only on skills, push them toward structural factors.

**Grouping:** 2 per group

#### Horse or Coal?

Lowrey's framework: horses were replaced by tractors and never adapted. Coal was supposed to be replaced by efficiency, but the Jevons paradox meant demand actually increased. Radiologists were predicted to be obsolete by 2021, but they survived because improved imaging created new demand AND FDA regulation slowed AI deployment.

**Discuss with your partner:** Apply the horse-vs-coal test to your own future career or field of study. Are you more likely to be enhanced by AI or replaced by it? What about people who don't go to college? Lowrey says factors beyond skill matter: regulation, professional licensing, consumer preferences. What structural factors, beyond just being good at your job, might protect or endanger your career?

**Capture your key points:**

_Expected response: What did you and your partner discuss? Where did you agree or disagree?_

> **Facilitation notes**
>
> Ask pairs to share their horse-vs-coal self-assessments. Listen for structural factors beyond individual skill. Bridge to Round 4: "Whether AI helps or hurts workers isn't just about technology or skill. It's about choices: who makes them, and in whose interest."

#### Share Out

Your instructor will ask a few pairs to share what they discussed. Listen for ideas that challenge or extend your own thinking.

### Stage 7: Round 4: Who Decides?

**Timing:** planned 10m

_New partners discuss governance and worker agency_

> **Facilitation notes**
>
> Cassidy's piece is short but sharp. The electrician vs Amazon example shows that the same technology produces opposite outcomes depending on who controls the implementation. If students say "the government should regulate," push for specifics. If they say "the market will sort it out," ask them about the 4,000 Block employees. The governance vacuum is the point.

**Grouping:** 2 per group

#### Who Decides?

Cassidy describes two uses of AI: one that helps electricians diagnose problems faster (pro-worker) and another that surveils Amazon warehouse employees' every movement (anti-worker). Same technology, opposite effects. The difference isn't the AI. It's who decided how to deploy it and in whose interest.

Thompson's Block employees had no say in Dorsey's decision to fire them. Lowrey notes that radiologists survived partly because FDA regulation slowed AI deployment in medicine.

**Discuss with your partner:** Who should decide how AI is deployed in workplaces? The companies building it? The workers affected? Government regulators? What would "pro-worker AI" look like at Illinois, in your major, or in a campus job?
