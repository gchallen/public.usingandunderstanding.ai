# Creating Websites

**Instructor Guide** · 2026-04-14

Build a website with an AI site builder — brainstorm, build, share.

## Facilitation Notes

**Philosophy:** This lab puts students in the role of a product designer, not a programmer. The skill we're practicing is articulating what you want clearly enough that an AI can build it, and then iterating when the first attempt isn't quite right. That's a transferable skill whether or not they ever touch code again. Encourage students to think beyond profile pages: the most interesting results come from students who build something useful or solve a real problem.

**Logistics:**
- Pick one builder and have the whole class use it. Mixing tools costs you the ability to help from the front of the room.
- Options that worked or would work: [Replit](https://replit.com), [Lovable](https://lovable.dev), [Bolt](https://bolt.new), [v0](https://v0.dev), or Claude Artifacts. See the external tools chapter for how to choose and what each costs.
- Check the free tier the week before. These change often, and a tier that stopped being free is a dead lab.
- No setup should be required beyond creating an account
- Students submit their published project URL at the end of stage 3, and we browse them in stage 4
- The link-submission component uses `kind: "basic"` so it accepts any live http(s) URL (no AI-chat extraction)

**Pacing:**
| # | Stage | Duration | Cumulative |
|---|-------|----------|------------|
| 0 | Brainstorming | 10m | 10m |
| 1 | Share-Out | 10m | 20m |
| 2 | Build Your Site | 35m | 55m |
| 3 | Gallery | 15m | 70m |
| 4 | Feedback | 5m | 75m |

**Tips:**
- During brainstorming, push students away from "I don't know" toward anything concrete — a hobby, a class project, a side interest. The worst website is the one that never got started because the student couldn't decide what to build.
- During share-out, surface 3–4 distinctive ideas. Good prompts: "What's one you haven't heard anyone else mention?" "Which one surprised you?"
- During the build stage, walk the room. The most common failure mode is students fighting the AI over small details instead of asking for what they actually want. When a student is stuck, ask "what are you trying to accomplish?" and help them re-describe it from scratch.
- Some students will want to tweak CSS or HTML directly — that's fine, but gently redirect them back to the prompt-based workflow. The pedagogical point is that natural-language description is sufficient.
- During the gallery, click through each submission and give each site 60–90 seconds. Call on the student whose site you're showing to narrate briefly: "What is it? What were you trying to build?"

## Before class

### Creating Websites

#### Today's Plan

Today you'll use AI to build something many people assume you need to know how to code to build: a website. It could be a site that solves a problem, something useful for you or your friends, a personal page, a game, or anything else you can imagine.

You'll brainstorm what you want to build, share ideas, build it with an AI site builder, and close with a gallery where we'll look at what everyone made. You won't write a single line of HTML, CSS, or JavaScript. The AI builds it; your job is to decide what you want and give it good instructions.

## Activity

### Stage 1: Brainstorming

**Timing:** planned 10m

_Decide what you want to build_

> **Facilitation notes**
>
> Students brainstorm individually. The goal is that everyone leaves this stage with *one concrete idea* they can start building in stage 2. Discourage over-scoping — the best submissions will be focused, not ambitious. If a student is stuck, ask "what's something that annoys you that a simple website could fix?" or "what's something your friend group always argues about that a site could settle?"

#### What kind of website do you want to build?

Think about a website you'd actually want to exist. It doesn't have to be about you. Some ideas:

- **Something that solves a problem** — splitting costs with roommates, picking where to eat, tracking who owes what in a group
- **Something useful for a group you're part of** — a club info page, a resource list for a study group, a schedule for a shared space
- **A quiz, game, or interactive thing** — a personality quiz, a trivia game, a "which X are you?" generator
- **A hobby or interest site** — something you're into (music, sports, a game, a collection)
- **A review or recommendation site** — books, movies, restaurants, anything with opinions
- **A personal landing page or mini portfolio** — who you are, what you make, a few links
- **Something weird or fun** — a fake business, a fan shrine, an elaborate joke, a tribute to something niche

You're going to build this in about 35 minutes with an AI assistant, so keep the scope small. A few pages at most. Not a whole app.

### Stage 3: Build Your Site

**Timing:** planned 35m

_Individual work with your AI builder_

> **Facilitation notes**
>
> Main work stage. Walk the room. Common problems: (1) scope creep — students add feature after feature and never finish; (2) fighting the AI over small details — redirect to "describe what you want overall and let it rebuild"; (3) accepting the first output without iterating. Good prompts for stuck students: "What's the one thing you want people to see first?" "If someone landed on your site with no context, what would you want them to do next?"

Open the site builder your instructor named and start a new project. Describe what you want to build — not how to build it.

**Getting started:**
1. Sign in to the builder (create an account if you don't have one)
2. Start a new project and choose the AI-assisted option
3. Describe your website in natural language: *"Build me a site that ..."*
4. Let it generate the site, then iterate: ask for changes, new sections, different colors, etc.

**Tips for working with the AI agent:**
- **Describe outcomes, not code.** Say "I want a hero section with my name and a tagline" not "add a div with class hero".
- **Iterate in small steps.** Ask for one change at a time and look at the result.
- **Preview often.** Most builders show a live preview. Check it after every change.
- **Re-describe when you're stuck.** If the agent is taking your site in a weird direction, stop and describe the whole thing again from scratch.
- **Don't overthink the content.** Placeholder text and emoji are fine. You can polish later if there's time.

When your site is working, publish it. The builder will give you a public URL. You'll submit it below.
