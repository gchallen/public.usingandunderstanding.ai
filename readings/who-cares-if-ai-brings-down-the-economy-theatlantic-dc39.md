## 1. "$650 billion on the AI build-out"

To put this in perspective: the entire annual budget of the U.S. Department of Education is about $80 billion. The AI industry is spending eight times that amount in a single year on infrastructure, much of it to train and run the same kinds of models you use in this course. The scale of investment shapes what gets built and for whom.

## 2. "computing power to throw at training AI models"

This is the "scaling laws" hypothesis in action: larger models trained on more data with more compute tend to perform better. The billions being spent on data centers are a bet that this relationship will continue to hold. Whether it does -- or whether we hit diminishing returns -- is one of the most important open questions in AI research right now.

## 3. "Who cares about better chatbots if you're about to retire and a crash wipes out your 401(k)?"

This is the central ethical question of the article and one worth applying to AI more broadly: who benefits from AI development, and who bears the costs? The people making decisions about AI investment are largely insulated from the consequences of failure. As students entering a workforce shaped by these decisions, this asymmetry affects you directly.
