# The Hypocrisy at the Heart of the AI Industry

The article itself is not included here. These are the course's annotations,
each quoting the phrase it was attached to.

## 1. ""If nobody uses your product, then it doesn't matter that you stole all the content.""

This is a remarkably candid statement of Silicon Valley's "move fast and break things" philosophy applied to intellectual property. Notice how this framing treats copyright infringement as a business risk to be managed, not an ethical line to be respected. Does the potential social benefit of a technology justify taking others' work without permission?

## 2. "We can train on your work, but you can't train on ours."

This asymmetry is worth sitting with. The same companies that argue their training on copyrighted books is "fair use" explicitly forbid others from using their outputs the same way. When you use AI tools in your own work, you're participating in this system whether you've thought about it or not.

## 3. "near-exact copies of media they were trained on"

AI memorization is a key technical concept in this course. Models don't just learn patterns -- they sometimes memorize specific training examples verbatim. This undermines the "transformative use" argument that AI companies rely on for their fair use defense, because the output can be essentially identical to the copyrighted input.
