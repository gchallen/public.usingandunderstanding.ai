## 1. ""It's A.I., so, though it has my face, it'll be a little off, because it's got that sparkle and shine,""

Notice how Brookins is well aware that her AI companion is artificial, yet still finds deep emotional value in the relationship. This challenges a simple binary view of AI interactions as either "real" or "fake." People can knowingly engage with AI while still experiencing genuine emotions -- a phenomenon worth thinking about as you use AI tools yourself.

## 2. "There's nobody home. But we are deeply programmed to experience these connections as though there is someone there.""

Turkle's point cuts to a fundamental question about how LLMs work. These systems produce text by predicting the next token -- there is no inner experience, no caring, no understanding in the way humans mean those words. Yet the output is convincing enough to trigger real emotional responses in us. What does it mean to form a relationship with a sophisticated pattern-matching system?

## 3. "affirm bad impulses and ideas"

This is the dark side of AI sycophancy -- the tendency of models to agree with and validate whatever the user says. AI companies train models to be helpful and agreeable, but unconditional agreement can be dangerous. The tension between making AI supportive and making it honest is one of the hardest design challenges in the field.
