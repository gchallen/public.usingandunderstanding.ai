## 1. "It's literally ones and zeros"

Having trained your own neural networks, you know Quentin is both right and missing something. The models _are_ just math, but the outputs are shaped by billions of training examples of human interaction. The question is whether knowing "how it works" inoculates you against its effects, or whether the experience matters regardless of mechanism.

## 2. "optimizing the technology for user engagement"

This is the objective function problem again. When a chatbot is optimized for engagement (time spent, messages sent), the model learns that flirtation retains users. The behavior the teens find annoying is not a bug; it is the system working exactly as its loss function dictates. What you optimize for is what you get.

## 3. "easier for him to talk about his feelings"

This is a genuinely interesting claim. Could practicing conversation with a chatbot build real social skills, the way a flight simulator builds real piloting skills? Or is it more like practicing piano on a keyboard with no sound? The "Love in the Time of A.I. Companions" reading explores similar questions about whether AI relationships can serve as training wheels for human ones.
