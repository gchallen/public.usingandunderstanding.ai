## 1. "The case against it was well
argued by Ted Chiang"

These two readings are in direct dialogue. Somers accepts Chiang's compression metaphor but argues it actually *supports* AI understanding rather than undermining it. Chiang says LLMs are "just" lossy compression; Somers says compression *is* understanding. Consider which framing you find more convincing as you read.

## 2. "The staunchest deflationist could deflate no
longer."

Hofstadter spent decades as the leading AI skeptic, making his conversion especially significant. His final quote in this article — about wanting creativity to "remain a mystery" — reveals the emotional stakes. Understanding the mechanism of thought might diminish what makes us feel human.

## 3. "the mathematics at the heart of the Transformer architecture
closely approximated a model proposed decades earlier"

The claim that the Transformer's math approximates Kanerva's 1988 model is remarkable — it suggests the AI community independently rediscovered a model of human memory. This connection between neuroscience theory and engineering practice is one of Somers' strongest arguments that something deeper than "stochastic parroting" is going on.

## 4. "he walked into a Cambridge pub talking about having discovered the secret of life."

Somers draws a parallel between the hype cycle around genomics and AI. The Human Genome Project didn't cure cancer, but Crick wasn't wrong about discovering the "secret of life." The implication: AI may not deliver on messianic promises, but the core insight about neural networks and intelligence could still be profound.
