## 1. "rule-following teacher's pets"

This is the tension at the heart of RLHF (reinforcement learning from human feedback). The same post-training process that makes models safe, helpful, and well-behaved also flattens their output. When you've used ChatGPT or Claude, have you noticed how the writing tends toward a particular "voice"? That's partly a consequence of this optimization.

## 2. ""next-token prediction""

This is a clear, concise explanation of the core mechanism behind all large language models. The key insight is that LLMs don't "understand" language the way humans do -- they predict what comes next based on statistical patterns learned from enormous amounts of text. Everything else, including what looks like creativity, emerges from this simple mechanism.

## 3. "specificity of a life"

This gets at a fundamental question about AI understanding: can a system that has never experienced the physical world produce genuinely meaningful descriptions of it? LLMs have processed billions of words about sunsets, heartbreak, and exhaustion, but they have never felt any of them. Whether this matters -- whether understanding requires experience -- is one of the deepest open questions in AI.
