## 1. "making them bigger"

This is the "scaling hypothesis" in action: the idea that making models larger -- with more parameters, more data, and more compute -- reliably makes them more capable. It is the driving force behind the data center boom described here. Whether this approach will continue to yield improvements, or whether we will hit diminishing returns, is one of the biggest open questions in AI.

## 2. "bubble"

Every time you use a chatbot, it costs the company real money in electricity and compute. Most AI companies are currently spending far more than they earn. If AI fails to generate enough economic value to justify these costs, the massive infrastructure buildout described in this article could end up as stranded assets -- much like the fiber-optic cables of the dot-com bust.

## 3. "almost entirely Black"

The pattern of placing polluting infrastructure near Black and low-income communities is a well-documented environmental justice issue. AI's enormous energy demands are now reproducing this pattern, raising the question: who bears the physical costs of the AI systems that primarily benefit wealthier, whiter communities?
