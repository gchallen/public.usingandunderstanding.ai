# AI Is Nothing Like a Brain, and That's OK

Yasemin Saplakoglu · Quanta Magazine · 2025-04-30

The article itself is not included here. These are the course's annotations,
each quoting the phrase it was attached to.

## 1. "logic device: It either turns on or it doesn't"

This is exactly the model you explored on Tuesday. Each artificial neuron in the digit network takes inputs, multiplies by weights, sums them up, and applies an activation function -- producing an output that's either "on" (fires) or "off" (doesn't). McCulloch and Pitts' 1943 idea is still the foundation of every neural network, including the one you built.

## 2. ""caricatures of biological neurons,""

Think about the neuron you explored on Tuesday. It had a handful of inputs, one set of weights, and a single activation function. A real biological neuron has 130 inputs and 200 outputs just to say "I've stretched" — and it encodes *how much* through firing frequency, not just yes or no. The gap between what you built and what's in your head is enormous.

## 3. ""weight" — a number that determined the input's significance to the final output"

These are the same weights you adjusted by hand on Tuesday. When you changed a weight in the digit network, you were doing manually what training does automatically — finding values that make the network's predictions more accurate.

## 4. ""basically just linear algebra,""

This is what you saw on Tuesday: multiply inputs by weights, add them up, apply an activation function. That's it. Every AI system — from the digit network to GPT-4 — is built from this same simple operation, repeated billions of times.

## 5. "between five and eight layers of nodes"

Let that sink in: about 1,000 artificial neurons to approximate *one* biological neuron. Tuesday's digit network had roughly 13,000 artificial neurons total. A single cubic millimeter of brain tissue has about 50,000 biological neurons — each one as complex as a small deep network. Your brain has 86 billion of them.

## 6. ""incredibly energy efficient,""

For reference, training GPT-4 is estimated to have consumed roughly 50 gigawatt-hours of electricity — enough to power about 5,000 homes for a year. Your brain does something arguably more impressive on the energy budget of a dim lightbulb, 24 hours a day, for your entire life.

## 7. "really different information-processing object"

This is the key takeaway. AI started by trying to copy the brain, but what emerged is something genuinely new — not better or worse, just different. The digit network you explored Tuesday doesn't work *like* your brain. But it works. Understanding *why* it works despite being so different from biology is one of the deepest questions in the field.
